# Farmer Dalal, 48

**Location:** Doukkala plain, near Sidi Bennour, Morocco
**Farm:** 4,5 hectares, irrigated, family-owned
**Crops:** Sugar beet (dominant), soft wheat, forage maize, some alfalfa
**Livestock:** Yes: dairy cattle, improved breed
**Tech comfort:** Uses a smartphone daily. WhatsApp for coordinating with buyers and family. Has never used a satellite data tool.

## A day in her life
Checks the irrigation canal schedule. Walks the beet fields. Calls her son in Casablanca about sugar beet delivery prices. Worries about whether the water allocation will be enough this season.

## Her farm in context
At 4,5 hectares, Dalal is a typical Doukkala smallholder, 89% of farms in thge region are under 5 hectares, and 60,5% are under 2 hectares.

## Her current rotation
Sugar beet → wheat → forage maize. This is the most common rotation in the Doukkala irrigated perimeter. Sugar beet dominates because of government subsidies and contracts with COSUMAR (Morocco's sugar group), which allocated 18,500 hectares to Doukkala for the 2026/2027 campaign. For Dalal, beet is her cash crop, it pays the bills.

## Her worries
- **Water scarcity is her #1 concern.** Doukkala farmers consistently rank water management as their top priority, ahead of input costs and labor. She has lived through several years of severe drought.
- **She plants beet too often.** Because beet is profitable and subsidised, she rotates it every 2–3 years. She knows this depletes soil and builds up pests, but she doesn't know what alternative would still pay the bills .
- **Her soil feels "tired."** Research confirms that soil organic matter in Doukkala is low, which harms soil quality and productivity . She can feel it but can't measure it.
- **She can't afford to experiment blindly.** A failed rotation costs a season. She needs evidence before changing.

## What she needs from us
- Proof that a different rotation would have held more water during drought years
- Evidence that soil health is declining — not just a feeling
- Something she can understand in under 2 minutes, on her phone, without jargon

## What will make her close the app
- English-only interface (she reads Arabic and French)
- Abstract scientific terms
- Anything that ignores water, it's the constraint that defines her farm

## THE HOOK
A farmer in Doukkala decides what to plant this year. Her soil has been changing for years, but nobody has ever shown her how.
## THE PROBLEM
Farmers choose crops without seeing how their soil and water have changed. Most tools are in English, full of technical charts. In rural Morocco, many farmers read little, and those who do read Arabic.
## THE SOLUTION
Soil Memory turns NASA satellite data into something a farmer can understand without reading. She taps her field on a satellite map. The app shows two traffic lights: one for her vegetation, one for her soil moisture. Green is better, yellow is stable, red is getting worse. And she can press a button to hear the result in Arabic.
## THE DATA
Behind the lights is real NASA data: MODIS vegetation and SMAP soil moisture, monthly from 2018 to 2025, for five Moroccan agricultural regions: El Gharb, Tadla, Doukkala, El Haouz and Souss.
## ROTATION TEST
Then she can test a decision before planting. She picks three crops for two different plans, from 36 options: cereals, vegetables, legumes, trees. The app compares their estimated effect on soil, water pressure and disease risk, and it hides crops that don't suit her region.
## HONESTY
We are clear about the limits. Satellites can't measure what a rotation does to the soil, so those numbers are estimates based on agronomy principles, shown in the app and waiting for validation by agronomists. Our results come from the nearest of five reference points, not the exact field.
## STEPS
Next we will validate the numbers with agronomists, extend the data to any field, and test the app with real farmers and agricultural advisors.
## CLOSE
Soil Memory doesn't ask a farmer to understand data. It lets hIM see, and hear, what her land remembers.

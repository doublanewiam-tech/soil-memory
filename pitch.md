## THE PROBLEM — universal
"When soil degrades, nothing dramatic happens.
No alarm. No siren. The field still looks green.
But slowly, invisibly, it loses its ability to hold water,
store carbon, and feed the next generation.
By the time a farmer sees it, it's already too late."

## THE GAP — why nobody can see it
"Soil degradation is slow, invisible, and expensive to measure.
Lab tests cost money. Consultants cost money.
For most of the world's farmers, soil health is a guessing game."

## THE INSIGHT — the turn
"But here's what we realized: soil has a memory.
Every season leaves a mark — in its carbon, its moisture, its structure.
And for 20 years, NASA satellites have been recording that memory
for every field on Earth. Nobody has been reading it for farmers."

## THE USER — Dalal, grounded in reality
"Take Dalal. She farms 4.5 hectares in the Doukkala plain of Morocco.
Water is her constraint. She plants sugar beet every other year
because the subsidy makes it safe. But her soil is paying the price.
She can feel it. She just can't see it."

## THE DEMO — show, don't tell
"Here's Dalal's field. Here's ten years of her soil memory.
It's declining — less water held, less carbon stored.
Now she tests a rotation with fava beans instead of repeat beet.
The trend flips. She sees this before planting a single seed."

 ## THE CLOSE — position against the competition
"Existing tools monitor soil today.
We show farmers the trajectory — and what to do about it.
Free, open source, built on NASA data."
